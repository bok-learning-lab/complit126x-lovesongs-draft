# Workshop Overview

*Unit II: Voice, Style, and Form — Workshop Session*

---

## Where We Left Off

Before this session, you ran the spider chart and saw what a purely numerical representation of a poem produces when it's used to generate lyrics: something in the right emotional register, but without specificity. You may have also tried stuffing several poems directly into a prompt, or heard the generated output read aloud in a synthesized voice.

The through-line across all of those demos: the output works as a mood, not as a voice. The numbers capture *dimensions* of a poem — how melancholic it is, how romantic, how preoccupied with mortality — without capturing the specific images, syntactic habits, and formal moves that make those qualities feel like one particular writer's. That gap is real, and closing it is what prompt chaining is for.

Context engineering and prompt chaining are not magic. They are, as the reading argued, sequences of focused operations — each one doing one thing, each one passing something to the next. The question for today is: which operations, in which order, for your poet?

## Slotting in Your Mechanics

Using the framework as a scaffold, your job is to decide which operations from the [mechanics bank](/reading/prompt-chaining-guide#a-bank-of-operations) go where.

Some questions to guide the planning:

- What does each step need in order to do something the previous step couldn't?
- Where does the chain need a human decision — an annotation, a selection, a rewrite?
- Where does it loop? Where does it branch?
- What are you building toward generating, and what does that final step need in its context?
